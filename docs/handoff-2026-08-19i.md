## 2. What changed, in one paragraph each

**N82.** Damir's three colour calls. The canvas is `#f4f6f9`; the cream is gone. The
bubble hairline comes off — and the interesting part is that the recommendation was
to remove it in LIGHT ONLY. Light no longer needs it (the canvas alone gives 1.083
against cream-with-hairline's 1.055) and in fact the hairline pixel measured **1.007**
against the new canvas, i.e. it was erasing 1px of the bubble's own edge. In dark the
same pixel measured **1.281** against the bubble's own **1.120** — there it WAS the
edge. So it was built asymmetric, rendered side by side, and **he chose symmetric
removal with that number in front of him**. The dark security notice went darker and
more saturated — card `#0c1a4a`, edge `rgba(118,157,255,.35)` at 2.19:1 — and light
is provably untouched, which was his explicit constraint and is pinned from both ends.

**N19.** His dial was **connecting-only**, and #417 is why: during a community-bot
join essentially nothing reaches the shell, so a shared loading affordance would sit
still through the exact window it was hired for. Connectivity is a real pushed signal
the shells already recognise and already render as a title-state; this gives that same
state its visual half, driven by the same call so the two cannot disagree.

## 3. ★ What is now KNOWN that was not before

1. **The hairline was doing opposite jobs in the two themes**, and the numbers are on
   file. If dark now reads flat on the device, restoring dark alone is one line in
   `tokens.css` — the eight `box-shadow` rules that draw it were deliberately kept.
2. **★ Two N19 defects were invisible to every source-reading gate, and both were
   found by rendering the CSS.** (a) `var(--surface-action)` does not exist, and an
   invalid `var()` inside a gradient kills the whole `background-image` — the line
   rendered as *nothing* while the file read perfectly. (b) The hairline reset was one
   (0,2,0) rule placed high in `topbar.css`, where it silently lost to the variant
   `border-bottom` SHORTHAND below it and to the two `[data-theme="dark"]` overrides.
   This is the fourth batch running where the defect was in something adjacent to the
   change rather than in the change.
3. **★ jsdom's cssstyle DROPS a `border-bottom` shorthand containing `var()`.** So a
   light computed-style assertion on the topbar hairline passes vacuously. Dark uses
   longhands and IS observable — dark is pinned through the real cascade, light by
   source order, and the pin says so. Worth remembering: this suite's computed-style
   assertions are not uniformly trustworthy across shorthand vs longhand.
4. **Reply-to has no carrier anywhere** — not in `StreamMessage`, `SpixiMessage`,
   `SpixiMessageCode`, `ChatStreamMessage` or `FriendMessage`, and Spixi's C# contains
   zero occurrences of the word. The "zero-C#" claim is wrong about the scope, not
   just the amount: it is an **Ixian-Core** change. But the read found the shape of the
   fix — `ReactionMessage` is the precedent, `ChatStreamMessage` is the insertion
   point, its parser ignores trailing bytes so old clients degrade gracefully, and
   `FriendMessage`'s deserializer has been extended additively five times already.
5. **N14 was already built** under other numbers, and **N21 was already closed by
   #422** — the pattern ladder tops out at 0.1 against an old effective ~0.30, i.e.
   Strong today is about a third of what Subtle used to be. Both confirmed against a
   render, per the worklist's own instruction not to conclude it from the numbers.
