Desktop, not a mobile port

How a 2026 desktop app shows a chat list and a wallet, on Windows 11 and on macOS 27. All three directions use these patterns. First, today's v1 (the real src/demo/desktop.html): it works, but it is the phone layout in a wider window.

  1. The sidebar is a source list. Sections (Pinned, Agents, Chats) that fold, counts on the right, rounded inset selection. Not a phone list with a tint.
  2. Density follows the pointer. 36–40 px avatars and two lines by default, one-line compact rows as an option. Type stays at the #228 desktop step.
  3. Hover shows actions. Pin, mute, pop out and more appear on the row under the pointer; the right click menu has the rest.
  4. Select many. Ctrl-click and Shift-click select rows; one bar acts on all of them.
  5. Search is in the window chrome. Windows: the title bar search box (WinUI TitleBar). macOS: the toolbar and a scope bar. Both open the same palette.
  6. The wallet is a workspace. Balance and a 30-day chart on top, activity as a table with columns, filters and export, the selected payment in an inspector. No hero card.
  7. Details open in an inspector, beside the list, not by pushing a new screen. The list keeps its place.
  8. Platform chrome is native. Windows: Mica ground, a content layer with an 8 px corner, caption buttons. macOS: edge-to-edge glass sidebar, traffic lights, unified toolbar, capsule controls.

A · Deck

Who: the person who lives in Spixi all day and has twenty chats open in their head. What changes: Spixi works like a code editor or a mail client. Everything is a key away, several chats are open at once, and the app keeps running in the tray.

Signature: the palette. It finds chats, people, messages, files and actions, and it runs actions (>send 25 jonas), but every money action still ends in the native confirm.

  • Palette CtrlK — chats, messages, files, actions; Quick Look on Space
  • Native tabs and split groups — Ctrl\ splits; each group keeps its own tabs
  • Chat + info + files side by side — info is its own WebView, stripped of amounts
  • Pop-out windows, drag and drop, tray and toasts — all native, all through C#

B · Office

Who: a team that talks to customers, suppliers, drivers and agents, and pays and gets paid in IXI. What changes: the chat list becomes a shared inbox with an owner and a state for each conversation, contacts become a directory, and the wallet becomes a ledger.

It grows from the Spixi for Business concept renders ("Waiting on you", Treasury, agents, field devices) into the working screens a team uses all day.

  • Team inbox — Mine · Team · Unassigned, owners, waiting time, labels, team notes
  • Directory — people, organisations and agents as a table; files across every conversation
  • Ledger — the wallet as columns: reference, amount, fee, balance, status; requests to pay; CSV export
  • Tools dock — customer card, mini-apps and agents beside the chat; approvals are native

C · Stage

Who: anyone who uses Spixi to do something with someone: play, sign a lease, read an agent's report, settle a bill. What changes: a conversation gets a stage. Whatever is shared in the chat (an app session, a file, a payment request, an agent artifact) opens large beside it, and the chat stays a narrow column.

Why it is the strongest new idea I found: it gives the desktop a job the phone cannot do. Mini-apps get a real screen, files and agent artifacts get a real viewer, and the payment request is an object you read before the native confirm.

  • Stage — one sandboxed WebView per object, a provenance bar on each
  • Shelf — every object shared in a conversation, or across all of them
  • People strip — the list folds to avatars while the stage is open
  • Live objects — a game or a whiteboard shows who is on it right now
